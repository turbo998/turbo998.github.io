MY TAKE · SUPPLEMENT 4 PRODUCTION GUIDANCE 7 / 7

Default to Sonnet 5.5 & GPT-6.1 Sol for Interactive & Unattended Coding

How to route across the 17-model cohort based on latency, token budget, and hidden-test reliability.

✅ Good fit
  • Claude Sonnet 5.5 ($2.89, 21.7m, 99.00): Primary interactive Copilot CLI coding agent when developer wait time is the bottleneck (`21.7m` across 11 tasks, `1,310/1,310` hidden).
  • GPT-6.1 Sol ($1.71, 60 rds, 98.86): Default autonomous background agent where minimal tool-call chatter (`60` rounds, `9.3w` tokens) and sub-`$2` cost per 11-task suite matter most.
  • Opus 5.5 ($5.84, 99.68) & Fable 5.1 ($14.45, 99.77): High-assurance escalation tier when squeezing the last `0.7–0.9` quality points on complex architecture.
  • GPT-6 Luna ($0.17, 98.16): Ultra-low-cost prototyping (`A1–M2`) when paired with an automated pytest verification loop.
⛔ Not for
  • Paying Legacy Sonnet 5 ($7.87) or Opus 5 ($17.41) Rates: Strictly dominated on speed, rounds, cost, and/or score by `Sonnet 5.5` (`$2.89`) and `Opus 5.5` (`$5.84`).
  • Using GPT-6 Astra ($9.91) for Routine CLI Tasks: `GPT-6.1 Sol` (`$1.71`) achieves a higher overall total (`1087.50` vs `1087.00`) in fewer rounds (`60` vs `62`) at `17.3%` of the cost.
  • Unattended Deployment of DeepSeek-V4-Flash or Haiku 4.5: Both trigger runaway round loops (`420` and `341` rounds) and fail `60–71` hidden assertions.
  • Latency-Sensitive Pipelines on Grok 4.7 or Kimi K3: `181.2m` and `150.5m` wall times block interactive CI feedback loops.
