    • In 3D chameleon car paints, Morpho butterfly wings, jewel-beetle elytra, pearl nacre, dichroic camera lens coatings, and soap-bubble thin-film iridescence shaders, when light refracts at internal angle $\theta_t$ inside a dielectric film of refractive index $n_f$ and thickness $d$, the true round-trip interference optical path difference is governed by the normal wavevector projection $k_z = n_f k_0 \cos\theta_t$, giving $\Delta_{\text{OPD}} = 2 n_f d \cos\theta_t$ (not the geometric ray length $2 n_f d / \cos\theta_t$!), so the constructive peak wavelength shifts toward shorter wavelengths as $\cos\theta_t$: $$\Delta_{\text{OPD}}(\theta_t) = 2 n_f d \cos\theta_t = m,\lambda_{\text{peak}}(\theta_t) \implies \frac{\lambda_{\text{peak}}(\theta_t)}{\lambda_0} = \cos\theta_t = \frac{1}{\sec\theta_t} = \frac{1}{1 + u}$$

  2. Why Does a Longer Slanted Ray Path Inside a Thin Film Produce a Shorter Optical Path Difference ($\cos\theta_t$ Instead of $1/\cos\theta_t$)?

    • While the slanted geometric ray inside the film has length $2 d / \cos\theta_t$, the upper-surface reflection occurs further downstream along the interface, giving the top reflected wavefront a head start in air of $2 d \tan\theta_t , n_0 \sin\theta_i = 2 n_f d \sin^2\theta_t / \cos\theta_t$.
    • First-Principles Resolution (Normal Wavevector Projection): Subtracting the wavefront advance yields: $$\Delta_{\text{OPD}} = \frac{2 n_f d}{\cos\theta_t}(1 - \sin^2\theta_t) = 2 n_f d \cos\theta_t$$ In wavevector terms, tangential momentum $k_x$ is conserved across parallel interfaces, so only the normal component $k_z = n_f k_0 \cos\theta_t$ accumulates phase Difference $\Delta\phi = 2 k_z d$ across the film thickness!

Q2: How is internal cosine $\cos\theta_t$ computed directly from the external viewing cosine $\cos\theta_i$ without inverse trig functions?

  • By Snell's law $n_0 \sin\theta_i = n_f \sin\theta_t$: $$\cos\theta_t = \sqrt{1 - \frac{n_0^2}{n_f^2}\left(1 - \cos^2\theta_i\right)}$$ which requires only one sqrt and zero acos/asin calls in a GPU fragment shader!
